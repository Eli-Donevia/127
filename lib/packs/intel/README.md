# Competitor intel (`intel`)

Reads what competitors do on their own websites (offer, prices, booking, reviews shown, channels, video, ad tracking, email signup, and what they promise) plus a Shopify store's public product list, and turns it into the changes for the member's offer and site, a price range with sources, the gaps nobody near them fills, and what customers say in the reviews the member pastes.

## Workflows

| id | Title | Tier | Credits per run | Files |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `intel.clone_a_winner` | Clone a winner | free | up to 24 (5 sites) | playbook report (HTML), price chart (PNG + SVG), prices CSV |
| `intel.price_check` | Price check your market | builder | 33 | price chart (PNG + SVG), prices CSV |
| `intel.find_gap` | Find the gap | builder | 31 at 8 competitors, 35 at 10 | find the gap (HTML), who says what (CSV) |
| `intel.review_miner` | What reviews say | builder | 7 | what customers say (HTML), every review sorted (CSV) |

`price_check` finds competitors with `leads.find_businesses` (12 near the city), so it shares the business finder's sources: Google Places with the `places` key, OpenStreetMap, else sample.

**Find the gap** (10 min): kind of business, city, optional competitor websites (pasted ones first) and the member's own site. Picks 5 to 10 competitors with a website (OpenStreetMap only, never Google Places, so nothing from Places is stored), reads each one's homepage plus a services or pricing page and its FAQ, and counts 30 or so plain claims buyers look for ("Free quotes", "Weekend hours", "Text us", "Custom orders"), worded and chosen for the kind of business (no 24/7 calls for house cleaners, ordering instead of booking for bakeries). A claim turned down ("we don't offer financing"), asked as a question ("Do you offer packages?") or told in a customer's story on the page doesn't count, and each count keeps the sentence it came from. The page shows the 3 open lanes with a line the member could use only if it's true and how to check it, what only one or two say (with their words), what most say, who says what, and what none mention. "None" always means none of the websites we read, and the page says to check before claiming to be the only one.

**What reviews say** (10 min): the member pastes reviews as copied (theirs or a competitor's) and can add the business's own website. Reviewer names, profile lines, dates, star rows, buttons and owner replies are dropped, phone numbers and emails become [phone] and [email], and every quote is word for word. Each sentence is sorted into praise, complaints and asks by theme (the people, waiting, price, quality, cleanliness, calls and replies, booking, parking, hours, know-how, crowding, feel, choices, honesty, kids, pets). Own reviews get 5 moves for this week, lines to use and reply drafts for up to 3 unhappy reviews (with [your number] and [your name], nothing offered for changing a review, and no promise to change prices); a competitor's get openings, what to match and what their customers ask for. Review sites and social pages are never read: a Google, Yelp or Facebook link gets a plain "copy and paste them" line and no request is made.

## Tools

**`intel.read_sites`** (code, 1): in `{ sites: string (one per line or comma; "Name - site.com" works), max?: 1-10 = 5 }`; out `{ sites: [{ website, name }] }`.

**`intel.scan_competitor`** (code, 2; use in `each`; `viewMany` draws one table with the OpenStreetMap credit when a row came from it): in `{ site?: { website, name } or a leads place, website?: string, claims?: boolean }`; out `{ key, name, website, source, searchBy, siteStatus, siteNote, sample, platform, offer, prices: [{ amount, per, context }], booking: [names], proof, socials: [names], video, emailSignup, chat, ads, mobile, hours, headline, description, gaps: [chips], shop: { products, capped, low, median, high, types, titles } | null, claims?: { found: [ids], evidence: { id: sentence }, pages } | null }`. `claims: true` also reads up to 2 more of their pages (services or pricing, FAQ or about); the page text is cached 7 days and the claims are read from it each run.

**`intel.playbook`** (ai `plan`, 12): in `{ competitors: [scan_competitor outputs] (required), mine?: your website, what?: what you sell }`; out `{ patterns: [{ id, label, n, of, who }], moves: [{ title, why, how }] (5), summary, offer_line, mine, report (HTML asset), ai, sample }`. Without AI, the moves come from the patterns (what few competitors do first, then what most do that the member lacks).

**`intel.price_range`** (code, 1, wants `render`): in `{ competitors: [scan_competitor outputs] }`; out `{ groups: [{ per, n, sellers, low, median, high }], prices: [{ who, amount, per, context, source }], where, csv, chart (SVG asset), png (asset or null), credit, sample }`. When a competitor came from OpenStreetMap, the CSV's last row, the chart and the view carry the credit.

**`intel.pick_competitors`** (code, 3, wants `osm`): in `{ category (required), city?, sites?: one per line, mine?: your website, count?: 5-10 = 8 }`; out `{ sites: [{ website, name, source: 'pasted'|'osm'|'sample', address?, searchBy?, sample? }], category: { id, label, one, many, family }, city, notes, sample }`. Pasted sites first (review and social sites skipped with a note, the member's own domain left out), then businesses of that kind near the city that list a website (kit `findPlaces` with `google: false`, chains and known domains filtered). Error without a city or sites, or when nothing with a website is found.

**`intel.find_gap`** (ai `plan`, 12): in `{ competitors: [scan_competitor outputs with claims] (required), category?: pick_competitors category or a string, city?, mine?: your website }`; out `{ read, category, city, date, summary, lanes: [{ id, label, n, of, why, line, check, you, who, evidence }] (3), rows: [{ id, label, line, n, of, who, evidence }], youSay: [ids], mine, unread: [{ name, why }], notes, report (HTML asset), csv (asset), credit, ai, sample }`. AI picks the 3 lanes from the open candidates and writes why, a line and a check; its answer is dropped for the template (open lanes by weight) if it picks a non-candidate or uses a number that isn't a count. Error when fewer than 2 sites could be read. The CSV ends with a "How many of N" row and, for OpenStreetMap competitors, the credit.

**`intel.read_testimonials`** (code, 1): in `{ website?: the business's own site }`; out `{ site, testimonials: [{ text, page }], pages, notes, refused, sample }`. Reads the homepage and, if it links one, the site's own testimonials or reviews page (robots.txt respected, page text cached 7 days, failures not cached). Testimonial blocks only (JSON-LD reviews, blockquotes, testimonial or review sections, paragraphs under a testimonials heading), 8 to 160 words each, with signatures, cite lines and author names removed. A review or social host (Google, Yelp, Facebook, Nextdoor, Tripadvisor and about 60 more) is refused before any request, with `refused` set.

**`intel.mine_reviews`** (ai `write`, 6): in `{ reviews?: pasted text up to 40,000 characters, testimonials?: read_testimonials output, whose?: "My business" | "A competitor", business?, site?, refused?, sample? }`; out `{ whose, business, site, fromPaste, fromSite, date, total, counts: { praise, complaints, asks }, stars: { rated, average, counts } | null, praise: [{ id, label, about, n, quotes }], complaints: [...], asks: [{ text, themes }], words: [{ word, n }], themes, summary, moves: [{ title, why, how }], lines: [{ label, text }], replies: [{ review, quote, text }], notes, report (HTML asset), csv (asset), ai, sample }`. Counts and quotes come from the text (code); AI only writes the summary and moves, and its answer is dropped if it quotes words or uses numbers that aren't in the reviews. Writes nothing to the Pipeline.

## Real vs sample

- Competitor sites are always read for real (kit scan, robots.txt respected, cached 7 days); `.example` sites are samples.
- Shopify: `/products.json?limit=250` checked live on Oct 19 (allbirds.com answered 200 with products and variant prices, gymshark.com answered 403). We try it when the site runs Shopify or when the homepage has a bot wall; robots.txt is checked first; a refusal is reported, never guessed.
- **Live run (Oct 19):** `intel.clone_a_winner` on tartinebakery.com, zingermansbakehouse.com and allbirds.com: two sites read, allbirds' homepage blocked bots but its store answered (median product price from products.json).
- No review text is pulled from Google (Places terms require crediting authors and forbid storing content); nothing here reads Yelp, Facebook, Foursquare or any review site. "What reviews say" only reads what the member pastes and the testimonials a business shows on its own website.
- OpenStreetMap credit (D27): wherever a competitor found through OpenStreetMap shows (the pick list, the scan table, the price CSV and chart, the playbook and gap pages, the gap CSV), the credit and its link show too.
- **Live runs (Oct 19):** `intel.find_gap` on 8 bakery websites (7 read, 1 behind a bot wall; lanes: a satisfaction promise, text us, locally owned; custom orders found on 3) and on 6 house cleaning websites (4 read, 2 bot walls; lanes: a promised reply time, weekend hours, evening hours). These runs found and fixed: a charity named "God's Love We Deliver" read as delivery, a FAQ question and a customer's story on the page read as claims, and service-only lines shown to bakeries. `intel.review_miner` on merrymaids.com's own testimonials (16 read, names dropped, the "they lean happy" note shown); "don’t waste any time" with a curly apostrophe was first read as a complaint, now fixed. The OpenStreetMap top-up couldn't be tried live from the build container (Overpass unreachable), so it is tested with a fake map server.

## Keys

None required. `places` improves `price_check`'s competitor list (via `leads.find_businesses`), `render` makes the PNG chart, and `osm` (a harness setting, no key) lets Find the gap add competitors near the city from OpenStreetMap. Without it, Find the gap reads only the sites the member pastes, or sample businesses.
