How to keep your car clean between details

A full detail resets your car. The trick is keeping it that way for more than a week. Here is what I tell every customer in North Austin after we finish, and none of it takes long.

## Keep a small kit in the trunk

Put two microfiber towels, a small bottle of interior cleaner and a trash bag in a box in the trunk. When you spill coffee, you can wipe it up before it soaks in. Fresh spills come out with a damp towel. Old ones need a shampoo.

## Shake out the floor mats every week

Most of the dirt in a car comes in on your shoes. Pull the mats out once a week, shake them hard and put them back. It keeps sand from grinding into the carpet underneath.

## Wash the outside the right way

Skip the brush tunnel wash. Dirty brushes drag grit across the paint and leave fine swirl marks you can see in the sun. Use two buckets, one with soapy water and one with clean water to rinse the mitt, and wash from the top down. Never use dish soap. It strips the wax and leaves the paint unprotected.

## Dry with a towel, not the air

Water left to dry on its own leaves spots, especially with hard water. Use a large microfiber drying towel and pat the panels dry instead of rubbing.

## Park in the shade when you can

Sun bakes bird droppings and tree sap into the clear coat. If you can't park in the shade, wipe droppings off the same day with a quick detailer spray and a soft towel.

## Protect the inside from the heat

A sun shade keeps the dashboard from cracking and fading in a Texas summer. Wipe the dash and the door panels with a damp microfiber once a week so dust doesn't build up in the seams.

## Know when it is time for a detail

If the seats feel sticky, the windows haze up in the morning or the car smells even after you clean it out, it is time for a full detail. Leave it too long and the next detail takes more time.

Keep these habits and your car stays close to that just-detailed feeling for weeks instead of days.
