# Call Notes to Notion

*Turn every customer call into a clean Notion note and a dated next step, so you listen during the call instead of typing.*

**Workflow recipe** · Build track · Builder · 40 minutes

**You walk away with:** A Notion Calls database, a saved extraction prompt and a five-minute after-call routine that puts every next step in your Pipeline Tracker.

**Use it:** Use it before your first customer interview or sales call, then run the after-call routine within an hour of every call.

**Pairs with:** Automation Starter Pack (playbook), Validation Interview Scripts (playbook)

**When this happens:** A customer call ends

**This happens:** A clean summary sits in your Notion Calls database and the next step is dated in your Pipeline Tracker

**Tools:** Granola, Notion, Claude · **Cost:** Free plans available. Granola's built-in Notion sync needs a paid plan; copy and paste works free. Check current pricing. · **Setup:** about 30 minutes · **Tool details checked:** 2026-10

Every customer call is research. Most founders lose it. They type during the call, miss what the person meant, then forget the next step.

This recipe fixes that. Granola takes the notes. A prompt pulls out what matters. Notion keeps it, and your tracker holds the next step.

## What this does

Granola writes notes from your call while you listen. When you hang up, you paste the notes into Claude with one prompt. The result goes into a Notion Calls database. The next step and date go into your Pipeline Tracker.

## Before you start

- [ ] Granola installed on your Mac, Windows laptop or iPhone, with your calendar connected.
- [ ] A free Notion account with your HQ page.
- [ ] Claude open in a browser or the app. The free plan works.
- [ ] Your Pipeline Tracker open.
- [ ] The permission line below, ready to say.

> Free plan heads-up: Granola's free plan shows only your last 30 days of notes in the app. Its built-in Notion sync is a paid feature. So on the free plan, move each call into Notion the same day, by copy and paste.

## Build your Calls database

1. In Notion, open your HQ page and add a full-page database called Calls.
2. Add the properties in the table below. One page per call.
3. Make a view sorted by Next step date, soonest first.

| Property | Type | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | Title | Maya R., tutoring parent |
| Call date | Date | [date] |
| Call type | Select | Discovery / Sales / Check-in |
| Problem in their words | Text | "He freezes on word problems." |
| Would pay for | Text | Weekly sessions before tests |
| Next step | Text | Send three time options |
| Next step date | Date | [date] |

## Before the call

1. Start your notes in Granola when the call begins.
2. Ask permission in the first minute, using the line below.
3. If they say no, don't record. Take short notes by hand instead.

**Permission line** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Before we start, is it OK if I use an app to take notes? It lets me listen instead of typing. I only use the notes to remember what you told me.
```

> Recording and transcription rules differ by state and country. Some places need everyone on the call to agree. Ask every time, and check your local rules or ask a professional.

## During the call

- Listen. Jot three to five rough words when they say something important. Granola builds the summary around your notes.
- Ask about what they did last time the problem happened, not what they might do. The Customer Interview Kit has the questions.
- Before you hang up, agree on one next step and a date. Say it out loud so it lands in the notes.

## After the call (5 minutes)

1. Let Granola finish the enhanced notes, then copy them.
2. Paste them into Claude with the extraction prompt below.
3. Read the answer against the notes. Delete anything they didn't say.
4. Create a page in your Calls database. Fill every property.
5. Paste the extraction into the page, with the full notes below it.
6. Copy the next step and date into your Pipeline Tracker.
7. Send your follow-up today. The Day One Stack's Follow up after the call prompt drafts it.

**Extraction prompt for Claude** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Here are my notes from a customer call with [name], a [type of customer], on [date]:
[paste notes]

Pull out:
1. The problem in their exact words. Quote them.
2. How they solve it today, and what it costs them in money or time.
3. Every objection or hesitation they raised.
4. Anything they said they'd pay for, and any price they mentioned.
5. The next step we agreed on, who owns it, and the date.
6. One phrase I could use word for word on my landing page.

Rules: Keep it short. Quote them directly where you can. If something isn't in the notes, write "Not discussed." Do not add, guess or round anything.
```

## Paid option. Send it straight to Notion.

On a paid Granola plan, connect Notion in Granola's Settings, under Connectors. Then share each note to Notion from that note's menu.

- Granola saves to its own Notion database. You can't pick your Calls database.
- It's one note at a time. Nothing syncs on its own.
- Share from the Mac or Windows app. The iPhone app can't send to Notion.
- Link Granola's database from your Calls page, or keep the copy and paste routine. Both work.

## No Granola? Use a voice memo.

1. Ask permission with the same line.
2. Record the call with your phone's voice recorder.
3. If your recorder makes a transcript, copy it. If not, write your notes within 10 minutes of hanging up.
4. Paste the transcript or notes into Claude with the same extraction prompt.
5. Finish the same way: Calls page, then Pipeline Tracker.

## Test it

- [ ] Run a five-minute practice call with a friend. Ask permission like it's real.
- [ ] Run the prompt. Every quote matches something they actually said.
- [ ] The Calls page has every property filled in.
- [ ] The next step and date are in your Pipeline Tracker.
- [ ] You can find the call again by searching their name in Notion.

## Keep it human

> Automate the repeat, never the relationship. Granola takes the notes. You ask the questions, you hear the answers and you send the follow-up yourself. If someone shares something private, leave it out of your notes.

## If it breaks

- Granola missed the call: check it has permission to use your microphone and computer audio, and start notes before you talk.
- The summary is thin: you didn't jot anything. Add a few rough words during the next call.
- Claude added things they never said: delete them and reply: "Only use what is in my notes."
- Older notes disappeared on the free plan: Granola free shows 30 days. Move every call to Notion the same day.
- The Notion button is greyed out on a paid plan: wait for the enhanced notes to finish, and share from the desktop app.
