# One-Page Offer

*Build an offer so clear and so good it feels almost too good to refuse, on one page you can paste into a DM, a bio link or a landing page.*

**Template** · Start track · Builder · 45 minutes

**You walk away with:** A finished one-page offer with all eight parts, a value stack, honest price framing and notes from reading it to three real people.

**Use it:** Start track week 3 after your interviews, Build track week 1 to sharpen an offer, or Grow track week 1 to rewrite your headline.

**Pairs with:** The One-Page Offer (playbook)

---

Your offer is the spine of the business. Get it on one page and everything else gets easier: your DMs, your bio, your landing page.

Keep your interview notes open. The best words on this page will be your customers' words, not yours.

## Start with the spine

Write one sentence. Everything else on this page supports it.

> I help [who] get [outcome] in [timeframe].

**Your spine sentence**
[Read it out loud. Would your customer say "that's me"?]

Too good to refuse doesn't mean lying. It means stacking real value, removing risk and framing the price so yes feels easy. If you promise it, deliver it.

## The eight parts

**1. Who it's for, and who it's not**
[For: one specific person. Not for: who you'll turn away.]

**2. The outcome**
[The result, in their words from your interviews. Measurable if you can.]

**3. The timeframe**
[How fast they get it. Speed is value: "back in 48 hours".]

**4. What's included**
[Three to five deliverables. Specific beats long.]

**5. Bonuses**
[Cheap for you, valuable to them. Solve the next problem they'll hit.]

**6. Guarantee**
[Free redo, money back or pay after delivery. Only what you can afford to honor.]

> *“When selling cost, frame it as the lowest possible number. When selling value, the highest.”*
>
> Eli

**7. Price**
[One number, said plainly]

**Cost, framed small**
[Per day, per week or per use: "$3 a day", not "$90 a month"]

**Value, framed large**
[Per year or per outcome: "worth $2,000 in new clients a year"]

**8. Why trust you**
[Real proof only: past results, your own story, a free sample.]

**The tiny next step**
[One action under a minute: "Reply YES", "Book 10 minutes", "Send me your resume"]

## Too good to refuse check

- [ ] The value stack makes my price feel small.
- [ ] I named what would stop them saying yes today, and answered it on the page.
- [ ] The risk is low enough that saying no feels like the bigger risk.
- [ ] It uses their words from interviews, not mine.
- [ ] The next step takes under a minute.
- [ ] I considered a different model: paid on results, first job free or pay after delivery.
- [ ] I can keep every promise, every time.
- [ ] No fake scarcity, no fake reviews, no fake deadlines.

> **Filled example: resume review for college seniors**
>
> Spine: I help college seniors get an interview-ready resume in 48 hours.
>
> For: college seniors applying to business and finance internships who are getting no callbacks.
> Not for: people who want a full career coach.
>
> Outcome: a resume that gets past the filters and gets you interviews.
> Timeframe: back in 48 hours.
>
> Included:
> 1. A line-by-line rewrite of your resume
> 2. A version tailored to one job posting you choose
> 3. A 15-minute video walkthrough of every change
>
> Bonuses: a LinkedIn headline and About rewrite, plus a cold email template for recruiters.
>
> Guarantee: don't love it? I redo it free. Still don't love it? Full refund.
>
> Price: $49, one time.
> Cost, framed small: less than $1 a week for a year.
> Value, framed large: a document that could land a summer offer worth thousands.
>
> Why trust me: I rewrote resumes for six classmates last semester, and four got interviews within a month.
>
> Next step: DM me "RESUME" and send your current version.

> Use your real numbers only. No results yet? Say so, and offer your first five a lower price in exchange for honest feedback.

## Read it to three people

Read it out loud to three people who match your "For" line, ideally from your interviews. Then ask, "What would stop you from buying this today?" If anyone asks what you actually do, rewrite it.
