# Customer Interview Kit

*Everything for 10 customer conversations in one doc: your problem sentence, who to ask, outreach messages, a timed script, red flags and a notes form.*

**Template** · Start track · Free · 45 minutes

**You walk away with:** A one-sentence problem, 10 named people, outreach you actually sent and a page of notes in their words for every conversation.

**Use it:** Start track weeks 1 and 2 before you build anything, or Build and Grow week 1 for the best-customer script.

**Pairs with:** Validation Interview Scripts (playbook)

---

Ten real conversations teach you more than a month of research. This kit holds all of it: the problem sentence, the people, the messages, the script and the notes.

Give it 45 minutes to fill Steps 1 to 3 and send your first messages. Each call then takes about 20 minutes with notes. Ask about the past. Never pitch.

## Step 1: Your problem in one sentence

Write the problem before you look for people. Use the words a customer would say, not industry jargon.

> [Who] struggles with [problem] when [situation], and today they [current fix].

**Who**
[One specific person. "College seniors applying to finance internships", not "job seekers".]

**Problem**
[What hurts, in plain words]

**Situation**
[When it shows up]

**Current fix**
[What they do or pay today]

**Your sentence**
[Put it together and read it out loud. Would they say "that's me"?]

> *“Focus on solving a problem before building a solution.”*

## Step 2: Find 10 people who have it

Names, not personas. Talk to people who have the problem now, or who paid to fix it recently. Not friends who will just be nice.

- Social posts: find people complaining about it on Instagram, TikTok or X. Comment first, then DM.
- Reddit: your city, your college or the hobby, like r/Sneakers. Message people who posted about it.
- Campus: clubs, career centers, dorm group chats and Discord servers. Ask a leader if you can post.
- Family friends: ask parents, coaches and old teachers, "Who do you know who deals with this?"
- Local businesses: walk in during a slow hour. Be respectful and specific.
- Every call: end with "Who else do you know who deals with this?"

> *“Talk to everyone, everywhere, every time.”*

## Step 3: Send the ask

Short, honest and easy to say yes to. You're asking to learn, not to sell. Never pretend to be a researcher, and never pitch after promising you won't.

**First ask** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Hey [name], I saw your [post/comment] about [problem].
I'm [first name]. I'm starting a small business around [problem area], and I'm trying to learn how people deal with it right now.
Would you be open to a 15-minute call or a few voice notes this week?
I'm not selling anything. I just want to understand it from someone who's actually dealt with it.
Totally fine if not. Thanks either way.
```

**Follow-up (once, 3 days later)** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Hey [name], bumping this in case it got buried.
I'd still love to hear how you handle [problem]. Even 10 minutes helps.
If now's a bad time, no worries at all.
```

**Ask for an intro** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Hey [name], quick favor.
I'm talking to people who deal with [problem] to learn how they handle it. I'm not selling anything.
Do you know one person who deals with this and might give me 15 minutes?
I can send you a short message to forward.
```

> Follow up once, not five times. A no is fine. Silence is fine. Move to the next name.

## Step 4: Run the call

Ask about the past, not the future. Never pitch. Write down their exact words. Want to record? Ask first, every time.

**Interview script (15 minutes)** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
OPEN (1 min)
Thanks for doing this. I'm trying to understand how people deal with [problem]. There are no wrong answers, and I'm not selling anything today.

CONTEXT (2 min)
1. Tell me a bit about you and how [problem area] shows up in your life or work.

THE PROBLEM (5 min)
2. Tell me about the last time you dealt with [problem]. What happened?
3. How often does this come up?
4. What's the hardest part about it?

HOW THEY SOLVE IT TODAY (5 min)
5. How are you solving this right now? Walk me through it.
6. What did you try before that? What happened?
7. What does it cost you, in money, time or stress?
8. What do you like and hate about what you use now?

CLOSE (2 min)
9. If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about this, what would it be?
10. Who else do you know who deals with this?
11. Can I follow up with you if I build something?
```

### Dig deeper when an answer is vague

- Can you tell me more about that?
- Why was that frustrating?
- What did you do next?
- How much did you pay for that? How much time did it take?
- When was the last time that happened?
- What would happen if you just didn't solve it? ("Nothing much" means the pain may be small.)
- Then stay quiet for three seconds. People fill silence with the honest part.

### Red flags: tick any after each call

- [ ] They complimented the idea. Polite, not proof.
- [ ] They said "I'd totally buy that." Future promises are cheap.
- [ ] They said "someone should make that" but never tried to fix it.
- [ ] I asked "would you..." instead of "when did you last..."
- [ ] I talked more than they did.
- [ ] I explained or pitched my idea.
- [ ] They're a friend or family member who wants me to win.

Two or more ticked? Count the call as weak evidence and tighten the next one.

## Build and Grow: talk to your best customers

Already selling? Talk to three of your best or most recent customers. No customers yet? Run the main script with three prospects. Same rules: past behavior, their words, no pitching.

**Best-customer script (10 minutes)** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Thanks for buying, and for doing this. I want to learn what made it worth it, so I can do more of what works.

1. What was going on when you started looking for this?
2. Why did you buy from me instead of something else?
3. What almost stopped you from buying?
4. What would you tell a friend it does?
5. What would make it a no-brainer to buy again or recommend?
6. Who else do you know who should have this?
```

> Their answer to question 4 is often a better headline than yours. Their answer to question 3 belongs in your FAQ. Quote anyone by name only with permission.
