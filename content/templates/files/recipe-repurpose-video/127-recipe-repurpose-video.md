*Film one 3 to 8 minute video and turn it into 3 to 5 short clips with hooks, on-screen text and captions, edited on your phone.*

Filming is the hard part. So do it once.

One good video holds three to five short posts. Claude plans the cuts. CapCut does the edit on your phone. You stay the voice.

## What this does

You film one longer video. Claude reads the transcript and plans 3 to 5 clips under 45 seconds, each with a hook and on-screen text. You edit them in CapCut, post each one natively and log what happens.

## Before you start

- [ ] CapCut installed on your phone.
- [ ] Claude open. The free plan works.
- [ ] Your 30-Day Content Planner open.
- [ ] Your three voice words from your Brand Sheet.
- [ ] One topic customers keep asking you about.
- [ ] Good light, a quiet room and your phone propped up vertically.

## Step 1. Film one video

- Film vertical, 3 to 8 minutes, facing the light.
- Pick one format: a tip list, a story, a before-and-after, or three customer questions answered.
- Make each point stand alone. Open each one with a clear first line, so it can start a clip.
- Pause for a second between points. Pauses make clean cut points.

> **Filled example: sneaker cleaning**
>
> Video: 6 minutes cleaning one pair of white leather sneakers.
> Clip 1: "Stop using dish soap on leather." The mistake and the fix.
> Clip 2: "Three things I use on every pair." Products on the table.
> Clip 3: "The midsole trick nobody shows you." One close-up technique.
> Clip 4: The before-and-after reveal, 15 seconds.
> Call to action on every clip: Comment CLEAN for prices.

## Step 2. Get the transcript

Many editors and phone apps can make a transcript. If yours can't, watch the video back and write a quick outline with timestamps. Claude can plan from either.

## Step 3. Plan the clips with Claude

**Editing brief for Claude** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
VIDEO EDITING BRIEF

I filmed a [length] video about [topic] for [who it's for].
My voice: [three voice words]. My call to action: [one action, e.g. Comment CLEAN for prices].
Transcript with timestamps, or my outline: [paste]

Give me 3 to 5 clips. For each clip:
1. Start and end time. Under 45 seconds. One idea only.
2. A hook for the first 2 seconds, under 10 words, using words I actually said.
3. Where to cut pauses or repeats inside the clip.
4. On-screen text: the hook, plus up to 3 short lines for the key points.
5. Which clip to post first, and why.

Rules: Use only what I said in the video. Don't invent facts, results, prices or claims. If a clip needs context a new viewer won't have, tell me what to add.
```

## Step 4. Edit in CapCut

1. Start a new project and add the full video.
2. Trim to clip 1's start and end times from the brief.
3. Cut every pause and repeat. The hook is the first thing they hear.
4. Add the hook as on-screen text in the first 2 seconds. Keep text near the middle, away from the edges where app buttons sit.
5. Tap Captions, then Auto Captions, then Generate. Fix every wrong word.
6. Export. Repeat for each clip.

> If CapCut asks you to join Pro when you export, something in the edit is a Pro feature. Look for the Pro icon and remove it. Auto captions can be one of them. If so, type the key lines as on-screen text, or use the caption tool in the app you post to.

## Step 5. Write the captions

**Caption prompt for Claude** (copy and fill the [brackets])

```
Write a caption for each clip below, for [platform].
Clip hooks and summaries: [paste from the brief]
My voice: [three voice words]. My call to action: [one action].

For each caption:
- The first line restates the hook in new words.
- One or two short lines of value from the clip.
- The call to action, once.
- Under [number] words. No emojis. No hashtags unless I ask.

Use only facts from the clip. No results, numbers or promises I didn't say. Give me two options per clip.
```

Pick the better option and rewrite it the way you talk. If a caption claims something you didn't say, cut it.

## Step 6. Post natively and log it

- Post on your main platform first. Repost elsewhere only if it takes under five minutes.
- Upload each clip inside the app you're posting to, and write the caption there.
- If you add music, use the app's own sound library.
- Space the clips out. One a day gives each one a fair test.
- After 48 hours, log each clip in your 30-Day Content Planner. Give a format three tries before you judge it.

## Test it

- [ ] Watch each clip with the sound off. The on-screen text alone makes sense.
- [ ] The hook lands in the first 2 seconds.
- [ ] Every fact in every caption appears in the video.
- [ ] Captions have no wrong words, especially names, prices and product terms.
- [ ] The call to action works: the link opens, or the keyword triggers your Comment-to-DM.

## Keep it human

> Automate the repeat, never the relationship. AI picks cut points and drafts captions. You decide what's true, rewrite it how you talk and reply to every comment yourself.

## If it breaks

- The clips feel random: the long video had no clear points. Plan 3 to 5 points before you film next time.
- Claude's times are off: it can only work from the transcript. Check each cut by watching it.
- CapCut asks for Pro at export: remove anything with the Pro icon, as above.
- Captions get words wrong: fix them by hand before you export.
- People swipe away in 3 seconds: the hook is slow or vague. Try a runner-up hook from your Hook Bank.

## Next step

After a week, find the clip that beat your average. Write three new hooks for that idea in your Hook Bank, then film the next video around it.
