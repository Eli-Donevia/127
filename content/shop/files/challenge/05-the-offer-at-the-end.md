# The offer at the end

The challenge earns you one honest pitch. Make it once, make it clearly, and let people say no without feeling cornered.

## Say it once

The best place is the last day's evening email and, if you run one, a debrief live two or three days later. Not in the day emails. Not in a pop-up on Day 2. People who trust that you'll keep your word about "once" finish more days, and finishers are the ones who buy.

## Make it the next step, not a different thing

The offer works when it continues what they built. "You built your page this week. [Offer] adds [the next three things]." If you have to explain why your offer relates to the challenge, the promise in lesson 1 needs another look.

## What to put in it

- What they built, in their words.
- What the paid thing adds, in plain lines. Not a feature list: what they'll be able to do next week that they can't now.
- The price, the billing (one-time or monthly), and how to cancel.
- Your refund policy, in one sentence.
- A deadline only if it's real. If the price really goes up on a date, say the date. If it doesn't, don't invent one.

## Finisher perks

A small, real perk for people who finish all five days: earlier access, a badge, a review of their work. Perks reward finishing, which is what you want more of. Never tie a perk to buying.

## What not to do

- No fake scarcity: "only 3 spots left" when there's no limit.
- No results claims: don't promise money, customers or outcomes. If you share your own numbers, add the typicality line from lesson 3.
- No pressure stacks: one email, one live, one reminder before a real deadline. Then stop.

## Prompt: write the offer

```text
My challenge ended with participants having: [their five things].
My paid offer: [name], [price, one-time or monthly], [what it adds], [refund policy], [real deadline or "no deadline"].

Write:
1. The last-day evening email (under 150 words) that names what they built, says what the offer adds in three plain lines, gives the price and refund policy, and closes with "If not, everything you built is yours."
2. A two-minute spoken version for a live, in my voice.
3. One reminder email for 24 hours before the deadline, only if the deadline is real.

No hype, no fake urgency, no claims about income, customers or results.
```
