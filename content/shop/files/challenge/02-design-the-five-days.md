# Design the five days

Work backwards from Friday. Write the thing people will have, then split it into five pieces where each piece is also real on its own. Nobody should end a day with only notes.

## The shape that works

- **Day 1: make it exist.** The ugliest working version, out in the world. Momentum beats quality on day one.
- **Day 2: make it better.** One improvement with a clear before and after (a sharper offer, a better photo, a cleaner page).
- **Day 3: line it up.** Get ready to show it to people: who it's for, where they are, what you'll say.
- **Day 4: go public.** One post, one story, one share. The hardest emotional day, so keep the task the smallest.
- **Day 5: ask.** Five real asks, sent one at a time. A no counts. Silence counts. Sending is the win.

## Every day has the same six parts

Fill this in five times. If a box is hard to fill, the day is too big.

| Part | What goes there |
|---|---|
| Your line | One sentence from you that sets the mood. Short enough to say in a video. |
| The move | What they do, in one sentence, starting with a verb. |
| The tool | The template, page, prompt or file that does the heavy lifting. They make choices; the tool does the typing. |
| Done when | A check anyone could verify. "Published", "5 marked sent", not "felt confident". |
| The card | What they share when they finish. A short label and their own result. |
| If you're stuck | The three reasons people stall on this day, each with a one-line way out. |

## Write the stuck lines first

The stuck lines are where finish rates come from. Guess the excuses before you hear them: "I don't have a name yet", "I don't like how it looks", "I'm scared to post", "I don't know my price". Each one gets a smaller version of the task that still counts. Posting to Close Friends counts. A screen recording counts. A price you can change Friday counts.

## Running it longer

Once five days works, you can stretch it. 127's Day One Challenge runs 14 days. Keep the same shape: every day still ends in something real, the hard days stay early, and the ask comes at the end. Add days only where people need more time, never to fill a calendar.

## Prompt: draft the five days

```text
I'm running a free five-day challenge. Promise: "Five days. [thing] live." It's for [who], and my paid offer afterwards is [offer].

Draft the five days. For each day give me:
- My line (one sentence I'd say on camera, in a plain, direct voice)
- The move (one sentence, starts with a verb, about 15 minutes of work)
- The tool (a template, checklist or prompt I should give them; write it out in full)
- Done when (something anyone could check)
- The share card label (four words or fewer, in capitals, like "DAY 1 · I'M LIVE")
- Three "if you're stuck" lines with a smaller version of the task

Day 1 makes it exist, Day 2 improves it, Day 3 lines up who to show it to, Day 4 goes public, Day 5 sends five asks. Every day must end in something real outside the challenge.
```
