# The pages and the emails

You need three pages and nine emails. That's it. Write them in this order, because each one borrows lines from the one before.

## Page 1: the landing page

One screen on a phone should answer: what will I have, when, how long a day, what does it cost.

1. **The promise**, big: "Five days. [Thing] live."
2. **The dates and the time cost**: "Free. [Mon date] to [Fri date]. About 15 minutes a day."
3. **What you'll have by Friday**: five short lines, one per day, each naming the real thing.
4. **Who it's for**, and who it isn't for. Saying who it isn't for makes the right people trust it more.
5. **The join form**: first name, email, and a separate unticked box for marketing email if you plan to send anything after the challenge.
6. **No catch**: "It's free. No card. On Friday I'll tell you about [paid offer] once, and that's it."
7. **You**, briefly: one photo, two lines on why you're running it. If you mention your own results, add: "One person's real numbers. Yours will differ. No results promised."

## Page 2: the joined page

Shown right after they join. Three things: what happens next ("Day 1 lands at 8am on [date]"), one small prep task ("Before Monday: write your [thing]'s name and one line on what it does"), and a link to send a friend ("Doing it with someone? Send them this").

## Page 3: the day page

One per day, or one page that changes. Your line, a 30 to 60 second video, the one button that opens the tool, the stuck lines, and the share card when they finish.

## The nine emails

Keep each one short enough to read on a phone in under a minute. One link per email.

| # | When | Subject | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | On join | You're in. Here's Monday. | What they'll have by Friday, the prep task, the friend link. |
| 2 | Day before, 6pm | Two minutes before tomorrow | The prep task again, and what time Day 1 arrives. |
| 3 to 7 | Each day, 8am | Day [n]: [the real thing] | Your line, the move in one sentence, the button. |
| 8 | Day 5, evening | What you built this week | Their five things, a thank-you, and the offer said once, plainly. |
| 9 | Two days later | Your next 30 days | What to do next on their own, the offer again with its end date if it has one. |

## Templates

**Day email:**

> Subject: Day 2: your offer, on your page
>
> [First name], yesterday you put it live. Today you make it worth buying.
>
> "[Your line]"
>
> The move: [one sentence]. About 15 minutes.
>
> [Button: Start Day 2]
>
> Stuck? [The most common stuck line, one sentence.]

**Offer email (day 5 evening):**

> Subject: What you built this week
>
> [First name], here's what exists now that didn't on Monday: [their five things].
>
> If you want to keep going, [paid offer] is [one sentence on what it adds]. It's [price]. [The deadline, only if it's real.]
>
> [Button]
>
> If not, everything you built is yours. Keep going.

Every marketing email needs your postal address and a working unsubscribe link at the bottom. Challenge emails they asked for are still worth sending with both.
