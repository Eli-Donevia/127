# Keep it clean

A few rules keep a challenge trusted, and keep you out of trouble. This is a plain checklist, not legal advice. If you run challenges at scale or with prizes, ask a lawyer where you live.

## Email

- [ ] Joining the challenge is consent to the challenge emails. Anything after the challenge (newsletters, future launches) gets its own unticked box.
- [ ] Every marketing email has your postal address and a working unsubscribe link.
- [ ] Unsubscribes are honored right away.
- [ ] Texts need their own unticked box, worded the way the law asks, and go only to people who ticked it.
- [ ] Don't let anyone under 13 sign up.

## Claims

- [ ] No promises of income, customers or results.
- [ ] If you show your own numbers or a participant's, they're real, and you add: "One person's real numbers. Yours will differ. No results promised."
- [ ] Testimonials are real words from real people, used with their permission.
- [ ] Counters show real counts.

## People

- [ ] Featuring someone (on a wall, in a reshare, on a live) needs their separate yes.
- [ ] If anyone under 18 shouldn't take part (because of what they'd sell or where they'd post), say so on the landing page.

## Prizes

- [ ] If there's a prize, you're running a contest or a sweepstakes, which has its own rules: written official rules, no purchase necessary to enter or win, and who's eligible. In the US, New York and Florida also require registration (and a bond) when the prizes add up to more than $5,000. The simplest path is no prizes: "no catch, no contest".

## The offer

- [ ] Price, billing and how to cancel are on the checkout page.
- [ ] The refund policy is written down and you follow it.
- [ ] Any deadline is real.

## Participants who start selling

If your challenge has people take money for the first time, add one line on Day 5: "If anyone pays you, be ready to deliver what you promised by the date you gave, or refund them right away."
