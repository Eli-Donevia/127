# Launch week, then the next one

A launch is seven days of telling people the same thing in different ways. Most people need to see it several times before they look.

## Before day one

- A waitlist page with the promise and a sign-up (lesson 1). Even 20 names makes launch day better.
- Five posts drafted, each showing a different file from inside the product.

## The seven days

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Email the waitlist: it's live, what it is, the link. Post a look inside. |
| 2 | Post the problem it solves, told as a short story from your own work. |
| 3 | Post one file from inside, fully, for free. Show exactly what they'd get. |
| 4 | Reply to every question you've had, publicly, as one post. |
| 5 | Email everyone who opened but didn't buy: the three questions people asked, answered. |
| 6 | Share what buyers made with it, with their permission. |
| 7 | Last email of the week. If there's a real launch price ending, say when. If not, just thank people. |

## Direct messages

When someone comments or replies with interest, send the link in a short, personal message. One at a time, from your own account. Never auto-DM people who didn't ask.

## After the week

- Ask every buyer one question: "What did you make with it?" Their answers are your next page's proof and your next product's idea.
- Look at what people asked for that wasn't inside. That's product two.
- When you have three products, make the bundle (lesson 3).

## Prompt: plan your launch

```text
My product: [one line], [price], for [who]. My waitlist: [number]. Where my audience is: [platforms].

Write my seven-day launch: the waitlist email for day 1, five post ideas (each showing a different part of the product, with the first line written out), the day 5 email answering the top three questions [list them], and a short DM I can send one at a time when someone shows interest. No fake urgency, no results promises.
```
