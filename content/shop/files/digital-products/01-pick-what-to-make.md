# Pick what to make

The best first product is something you already do over and over, written down so someone else can do it without you.

## Look for it in three places

1. **Questions you answer more than twice.** The answer, cleaned up, is a guide.
2. **Files you copy for every job.** Your proposal, your onboarding checklist, your shot list, your spreadsheet. Cleaned up, that's a template.
3. **Prompts you keep reusing.** The ones that work for your kind of work, with the blanks marked, are a prompt pack.

## The kinds, from fastest to slowest to make

| Kind | Example | Time to version one |
|---|---|---|
| Template | A Notion client portal, a pricing sheet, a Canva carousel set | A weekend |
| Prompt pack | 30 prompts for real estate listing copy | A weekend |
| Checklist or guide | "Open a food truck in Texas: every permit, in order" | A week |
| Preset or asset pack | Lightroom presets, motion graphics, fonts you made | A week |
| Short course | Five lessons and the files that go with them | Two to four weeks |

Start at the top. A template you finish this weekend teaches you more about selling than a course you finish next quarter.

## Test it before you build it

- **Ask ten people who'd use it** what they do today instead, and what that costs them in time.
- **Pre-sell or waitlist.** A page with the product's promise and a "Tell me when it's ready" form. Five sign-ups from strangers beats fifty likes.
- **Check what already exists.** If similar products sell, that's good news: people pay for this. Your angle is who it's for, not being first.

## Prompt: find your first product

```text
I do [your work] for [who]. Here's what I repeat every week:
- Questions I answer: [list]
- Files I reuse: [list]
- Prompts I reuse: [list]

Suggest five digital products I could make from these, each with: the product in one line, who buys it, what they'd use instead today, and how long version one takes. Rank them by how fast I can make version one and how clearly a stranger would understand what it does.
```
