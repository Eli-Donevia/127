# Price it

Price against the alternative, not against your hours.

## The three alternatives

Every buyer is choosing between your product and one of these:

1. **Doing it themselves from scratch.** What's their time worth? If your template saves four hours, it's worth a meaningful slice of four hours.
2. **Hiring someone.** If a designer charges for a carousel set, your carousel templates are a fraction of that.
3. **A free version.** There's always a free version. Your price pays for it being finished, specific to them, and in one place.

## Simple rules

- **Start with one price.** Two or three tiers are for later, once you know what people ask for.
- **Pick a number you can say without flinching.** Common ranges for a first product: $9 to $29 for a single template or prompt pack, $29 to $99 for a set or a guide, $99 and up for a course with files.
- **Bundles are your second product.** When you have three products, sell all three together for less than the three added up. Say what's inside; let the buyer do the maths.
- **No fake discounts.** A "was $99, now $29" price has to have been a real price people paid.

## Write down your numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Price | |
| Payment fees per sale (about 3% plus a fixed fee) | |
| Platform fee, if any | |
| What you keep per sale | |
| Hours to make it | |
| Sales to cover those hours at your rate | |

The last line tells you whether this product is worth making. If it takes 200 sales to cover your weekend, make a smaller product or charge more.

## Prompt: check your price

```text
My product: [one line]. Buyer: [who]. What they'd do without it: [alternative and its cost in time or money].
Draft price: [$]. Fees per sale: about [3% + $0.30]. Hours to make it: [hours]. My hourly rate: [$].

Tell me: what I keep per sale, how many sales cover my time, whether the price is easy to say yes to for this buyer, and two other price points with the trade-off of each. Don't use fake discounts or "value" totals.
```
