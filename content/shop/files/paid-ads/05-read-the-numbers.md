# Read the numbers and make the daily call

Look once a day, at the same time. Make one decision per ad. Then close the app.

## The five numbers

| Number | What it tells you | If it's bad, look at |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost per sale** (or per lead) | The only number that pays the bills | Everything below |
| **Click-through rate** (link clicks ÷ impressions) | Whether the ad makes people stop and tap | The first second (lesson 3) |
| **Cost per 1,000 impressions** (CPM) | How expensive the audience is right now | Usually seasonal; not much to do |
| **Landing page conversion** (sales ÷ link clicks) | Whether the page closes | The page (lesson 4) |
| **Frequency** | How many times the same person saw it | Above about 3 in a week, make new ads |

## The daily call

Compare each ad's cost per sale to your break-even (lesson 1).

| What you see after enough spend | Do this |
|---|---|
| Cost per sale under break-even | **Keep.** After a week of steady results, raise the budget by about 20% at a time, not double. |
| Cost per sale near break-even | **Keep, and improve.** Test a new hook on the same idea. |
| Spent your break-even with no sale | **Pause** that ad. |
| Lots of clicks, no sales | **Fix the page**, not the ad. |
| Few clicks | **New first second.** The idea might be fine; the hook isn't. |

"Enough spend" means about your break-even cost per sale for each ad. Before that, results are mostly luck.

## The weekly review

Once a week: which angle won (problem, proof, offer)? Make three new ads on the winning angle. Retire the loser. That's the whole loop.

## Prompt: daily check

```text
Break-even cost per sale: [$]. Here are yesterday's numbers per ad: [paste: ad name, spend, impressions, link clicks, sales, frequency].

For each ad: keep, improve, pause or fix the page, with one line of reasoning. Flag any ad that hasn't spent enough to judge. Then tell me the one new ad to make this week.
```
