# Your first test

Keep the first test small and simple, so when something works you know what it was.

## The setup

- **One campaign**, with the objective set to the action you actually want: purchases, leads or bookings. Not "traffic" or "engagement", which buy clicks and likes from people who rarely buy.
- **One audience, broad.** Your country or city, the age range that fits, and little else. Modern ad systems find buyers better than detailed interest picking does, as long as your tracking tells them who bought.
- **Three to five ads**, each a genuinely different idea (lesson 3). Not the same video with five headlines.
- **Tracking on.** The platform's pixel or conversions setup on your site, firing on the purchase or sign-up page. Test it with a real purchase before you spend.
- **Seven days, hands off.** Changing things every day resets the system's learning and teaches you nothing.

## Before you press publish

- [ ] The purchase or sign-up event shows up in the ad platform's events tool when you test it.
- [ ] Every ad's link goes to the right page, on a phone.
- [ ] Your break-even number (lesson 1) is written down.
- [ ] Your daily budget and the date you'll make the first call are written down.
- [ ] Your site's privacy policy mentions the ad pixel (lesson 7).

## Naming

Name everything so you can read results at a glance: `2026-11 test · broad · hook-price`. Future you will thank you.

## Prompt: plan the test

```text
I'm running my first Meta ads test for [offer] at [price]. Break-even cost per sale: [$]. Budget: [$] a day for 7 days. Location: [where]. Buyer: [who].

Write me a test plan: campaign objective, audience settings, how many ads and what each one should test, what to check before publishing, naming for each ad, and what I should look at on day 3 and day 7 (and what not to change before day 7).
```
