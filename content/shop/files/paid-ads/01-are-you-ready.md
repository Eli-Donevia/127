# Are you ready for ads?

Ads make something that already works reach more people. They rarely fix something that doesn't. Check these first.

## The four checks

1. **People have bought without ads.** Even a handful of sales from posts, referrals or messages tells you the offer makes sense to strangers.
2. **Your page works on a phone.** Most ad clicks are on phones. If it's slow or confusing there, the ad money leaks out of the page (lesson 4).
3. **You know your numbers.** Price, what one sale costs you to deliver, and what you keep.
4. **You have a test budget you're fine losing.** Your first week of ads buys information, not profit.

If one of these is a no, fix that first. It's cheaper.

## Your break-even number

The most a sale can cost you in ads before you lose money on it:

**Break-even cost per sale = price − cost to deliver − payment fees**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| Price | $60 |
| Cost to deliver (product, shipping, your time if it's a service) | $22 |
| Payment fees (about 3%) | $2 |
| **Break-even cost per sale** | **$36** |

If customers buy again, you can afford more for the first sale, but only once you've seen them actually come back. Start with one sale.

Write your number down. Every decision in lesson 5 compares against it.

## How much to test with

A first test needs enough spend to see a few sales, or clearly not see them. A rough guide: two to three times your break-even cost per sale, per day, for seven days. With a $36 break-even, that's about $70 to $110 a day for a week. If that's too much, test a cheaper offer or lower-priced product first, or keep growing without ads for now.

## Prompt: am I ready?

```text
I sell [offer] for [price]. It costs me [cost] to deliver. I've made [number] sales so far, mostly from [where]. My page is [link or description]. I could spend [amount] on a first test.

1. Work out my break-even cost per sale.
2. Tell me honestly whether I'm ready for paid ads, and if not, the one thing to fix first.
3. If I am, suggest a daily budget and test length, and tell me what result after seven days would mean keep going, change something, or stop.
```
