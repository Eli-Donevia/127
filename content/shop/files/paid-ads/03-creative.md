# Make the creative

The ad itself does most of the work now. The audience settings matter less than whether the first second makes someone stop.

## The first second

People decide in about a second whether to keep watching. Open with one of these:

- **The result, shown.** The finished thing, before you explain it.
- **The problem, said plainly.** "If your [thing] keeps [problem]..."
- **A surprising detail.** A number, an object, a place that doesn't belong.
- **A person talking straight to camera**, mid-sentence, like a message from a friend.

## Three angles to test

Make each ad a different reason to buy, not a different colour:

1. **The problem**: what's annoying, slow or costly without you.
2. **The proof**: the thing working, a customer's words (with permission), a before and after.
3. **The offer**: what they get, the price, why now (only if "now" is real).

## Formats

- Vertical video, 9:16, 15 to 30 seconds, with captions burned in (most people watch muted).
- A still image or carousel for products where one photo says it all.
- Creator-style videos (someone using it, talking to camera) often beat polished brand video. You can film these yourself or brief a creator.

## Briefing a creator

Send a one-page brief: the product, who it's for, the one thing to show, three hook ideas, what not to say (no promises, no medical or income claims), length, format, deadline, the fee and what you can use the video for and for how long. Paying for ad usage is normal; agree it before they film.

## Prompt: write three ads

```text
My offer: [offer, price]. Buyer: [who]. Their problem: [problem]. Proof I can show: [what you have].

Write three ad concepts, one per angle (problem, proof, offer). For each: the first line spoken or on screen (the hook), a 20-second script with what's on screen at each moment, the caption, and the headline. No promises of results, income or health outcomes. Then write a one-page brief I can send a creator to film the proof ad.
```
