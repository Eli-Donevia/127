# Google search ads

Search ads reach people who are already looking. If people type what you sell into Google ("emergency plumber near me", "wedding photographer Austin"), search often beats social. If nobody searches for it yet, stay on social.

## Check the demand first

Type your three best phrases into Google and look at what shows up. Ads at the top mean other businesses find it worth paying for. Google's Keyword Planner shows rough monthly searches.

## A small first campaign

- **One campaign, one location**, set to people in (not just interested in) your area if you're local.
- **Five to fifteen keywords** that mean someone wants to buy: "[service] near me", "[service] [city]", "[product] price", "hire [service]".
- **Negative keywords** from day one: "free", "jobs", "salary", "course", "DIY", "how to" (unless you sell those).
- **Ads that repeat the search**: if they searched "emergency plumber Dallas", the headline says "Emergency Plumber in Dallas".
- **Call tracking or form tracking on**, so you know which searches turned into calls or bookings.

## Weekly

Open the search terms report. Add anything irrelevant as a negative keyword. Move budget toward the keywords that bring calls or sales.

## Prompt: plan a search campaign

```text
I sell [service or product] in [area] for [price]. Break-even cost per sale: [$]. Budget: [$] a day.

Give me: 15 buying-intent keywords, 20 negative keywords, one responsive search ad (15 headlines and 4 descriptions) that repeat the searcher's words, and what to check in the search terms report each week.
```
