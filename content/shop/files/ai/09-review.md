# Review: the 10x pass

Most first drafts are fine. Fine loses. Run these on anything before it goes out: an offer, a page, a script, a plan. They find what is generic, unclear or untrue, and push it to the version you would be proud of.

## The 10x pass

**Use it when:** You (or your AI) finished something and it is "good enough". Run this before you ship it.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{work}}`: Paste what you made (for example: (your offer, page copy, script, email or plan))
- `{{goal}}`: What it needs to do (for example: Get gym owners to reply to a cold DM)
- `{{audience}}`: Who it is for (for example: Busy owners of small gyms)

```text
Here's something I made:

{{work}}

What it's for: {{goal}}
Who it's for: {{audience}}

Pretend you just finished this yourself, looked at it again and said: "Wow, I can't believe I thought I'd stop here." Now make it 10x better.

1. Be honest first: score it 1 to 10 on how well it does its job, and list the five biggest weaknesses. Be specific (quote the weak parts).
2. Name what a 10 out of 10 version would do that this doesn't. Think: clearer, more useful, more specific to the audience, less effort for them, more proof, better looking, more memorable.
3. Rebuild it. Deliver the full improved version, not a list of suggestions.
4. Show what changed and why, in a short table.
5. Give me one bold idea that would make it 100x, even if it's harder.

Rules: keep everything true. No invented facts, results, reviews or numbers. Don't make it longer unless longer is better.
```

**What you get back:** A 3 out of 10 score with five quoted weaknesses, the full rebuilt DM (opens on their specific gap and points to a sample made for them), a change table and one 100x idea.

## Is this high quality or AI filler?

**Use it when:** Anything an AI helped make. Catch the generic, robotic or padded parts before a customer does.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{work}}`: Paste the text or describe the design (for example: (your About page))
- `{{details}}`: Real details about your business (for example: Started cutting hair in my garage at 17, 400 clients, Tuesday walk-ins, $25 fades)

```text
Review this for quality the way a sharp, skeptical customer would. Tell me honestly if it reads like generic AI output.

{{work}}

Check for:
1. Generic lines that could be about any business. Quote each one.
2. AI tells: stock filler openers, lists of three adjectives, "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", em dashes everywhere, everything in perfect parallel lists, vague claims.
3. Padding: sentences that say nothing.
4. Missing specifics: where a real number, name, example, or detail from my business should be.
5. Things that sound untrue or overpromised.
6. Visual tells, if it's a design: stock-photo feel, default gradients, everything centered, no hierarchy.

Score it: human and specific (keep), mixed (fix), or generic (redo). Then rewrite the worst five parts using these real details from my business: {{details}}
```

**What you get back:** Every generic line quoted, AI tells flagged, a "generic, redo" score and a rewrite built on the garage story, Tuesday walk-ins and $25 fades.

## Five-second clarity test

**Use it when:** Before you publish a page, post, ad or pitch. If a stranger can't get it in five seconds, it fails.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{work}}`: Paste the page, ad or post (for example: (your homepage top section))
- `{{intent}}`: What you want them to understand (for example: We build booking websites for salons in a week, from $650. Book a free mock-up.)

```text
Act as a stranger who has never heard of my business. You see this for five seconds:

{{work}}

Answer as that stranger:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for? Is it for me?
3. What do I get, as an end result?
4. What should I do next?
5. Why should I trust it?

Then step out of character and compare your answers to what I intended: {{intent}}
For every gap, give me the exact rewrite that closes it. Keep the rewrites shorter than the originals.
```

**What you get back:** A stranger's five answers (probably "some salon software?"), the gaps against your intent and shorter rewrites that say "booking website, one week, from $650".

## Brand consistency sweep

**Use it when:** You have made a lot of things fast (posts, pages, emails, decks) and want them to look and sound like one brand.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{guide}}`: Your brand rules (for example: Name is "Northline", never "NorthLine Co". Calm and direct. Navy and sand. No exclamation points. Price $95 a visit.)
- `{{pieces}}`: Paste the pieces (for example: (Instagram bio, homepage hero, welcome email, flyer text))

```text
Check these pieces against my brand guide and make them consistent.

My brand guide (or the key rules): {{guide}}

The pieces:
{{pieces}}

For each piece:
1. Name: the business name and product names used exactly right?
2. Voice: matches the rules? Quote lines that don't.
3. Visual notes: colors, fonts, logo use, photo style (from what I described).
4. Claims and numbers: consistent across pieces? Flag any piece that says something different from the others (prices, dates, what's included).
5. Fix: the corrected text.

End with a short list of rules I should add to my guide so this doesn't happen again.
```

**What you get back:** Each piece checked, the name and price mismatches flagged ($85 vs $95), corrected text and new guide rules.

## Claims and compliance check

**Use it when:** Before any ad, sales page, email or post goes out. Catch promises that could get you in trouble or lose trust.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{work}}`: Paste the ad, page or email (for example: (your ad copy))
- `{{channel}}`: Where it runs (for example: Facebook and Instagram ads)
- `{{proof}}`: What you can prove (for example: 12 clients so far, 2 written reviews with permission)

```text
Check this marketing for claims that could mislead people, break platform rules, or lose trust.

{{work}}

Where it will run: {{channel}}
What I can actually prove: {{proof}}

Flag, quoting each line:
1. Promised results (money, customers, weight lost, rankings, timeframes) that I can't back up.
2. Income or earnings claims, or "results not typical" problems.
3. Testimonials, reviews, logos or numbers I can't prove are real and permitted.
4. Fake urgency or scarcity (a deadline or "only 3 left" that isn't true).
5. Missing disclosures: paid partnerships, affiliate links, automated messages, AI-generated images of people.
6. Before and after or health claims that platforms restrict.
7. Email basics: business name, mailing address and a way to opt out.

For each flag, give a safe rewrite that keeps the selling power. This is a common-sense check, not legal advice; say when something needs a lawyer.
```

**What you get back:** The results promise and the spots claim flagged, with rewrites that sell what you deliver (for example "We set up and run your booking page in 7 days") and a note on what to verify.

## Red-team it as a skeptical buyer

**Use it when:** Before a pitch, launch or price change. Hear every reason to say no before a real buyer says it.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{work}}`: Your pitch, offer or page (for example: (your one-page offer))
- `{{buyer}}`: The buyer (for example: Owner of a busy taco truck, skeptical of marketers)
- `{{alternative}}`: What they do today (for example: Their cousin posts on Instagram sometimes)

```text
Play my toughest potential buyer. Read this and tear it apart.

{{work}}

The buyer: {{buyer}}
What they use instead of me today: {{alternative}}

As the buyer:
1. Your gut reaction in one sentence.
2. Ten reasons you'd say no, from most to least likely.
3. The questions you'd ask before paying.
4. What would make you say yes today.

Then as my advisor:
5. Which three objections are fatal, and how to fix the offer (not just the words) for each.
6. What proof I need to collect next.
7. Why the buyer's current alternative might beat me, and the honest case for why I'm better.
```

**What you get back:** A blunt reaction, ten ranked objections, buyer questions, a yes trigger, three fatal objections with offer fixes, a proof plan and an honest comparison with the cousin.

## Pre-mortem

**Use it when:** Before you commit time or money to a launch, hire, tool or big change.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{horizon}}`: How far ahead (for example: 90 days)
- `{{plan}}`: The plan (for example: Launch a meal prep business and a fitness coaching offer and a TikTok channel at the same time)
- `{{resources}}`: Your time and money (for example: 15 hours a week, $800)

```text
It's {{horizon}} from now and this plan failed. Tell me why.

The plan: {{plan}}
My resources: {{resources}}

1. List the 8 most likely reasons it failed, from most to least likely.
2. For each: the early warning sign I'd see in the first two weeks, and what I'd do then.
3. Which failure is about doing too much at once, and what to cut so I focus.
4. The cheapest test this week that would tell me if the plan holds up.
5. A go or no-go checklist for the day I commit.
```

**What you get back:** Eight failure reasons with warning signs, a clear "do one thing" cut, a test for this week and a go or no-go checklist.
