# Offer: make it too good to ignore

A good offer does the selling for you. These prompts build an offer, a free lead magnet, a price ladder and a sales page that people find hard to say no to, without hype or promises you can't keep.

## Offer builder

**Use it when:** You know what you do and who for, and you want an offer that is easy to say yes to.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{service}}`: What you do (for example: 4 short promo videos a month for a gym)
- `{{buyer}}`: Who buys it (for example: Owner-run gyms with 100 to 400 members)
- `{{dream}}`: What they want, in their words (for example: "More people walking in for a trial without me having to post every day")
- `{{price}}`: Your current price (for example: $400 a month)
- `{{objections}}`: Why they hesitate (for example: Burned by an agency, no time to film, not sure videos work)

```text
Build me an offer that's hard to refuse. Honest, specific, no hype.

What I do: {{service}}
Who it's for: {{buyer}}
What they want most (in their words): {{dream}}
What I'd charge today: {{price}}
What makes buyers hesitate: {{objections}}

Work through four levers and show your thinking for each:
1. Outcome: what they get, stated as something concrete they can see (a finished thing, a number they control), not a vague benefit.
2. Believability: what proof I can show before they pay (a sample, a demo on their own business, a before and after, a process they can see).
3. Speed: how to get them a first visible result fast. What can they have in 48 hours?
4. Effort: what I can take off their plate entirely so they barely lift a finger.

Then:
- List every obstacle a buyer has, and for each one, the piece of the offer that removes it (a tool, a step I do for them, a bonus).
- Name each bonus. Say what it is and which fear it kills. No dollar "value" totals.
- Risk reversal that I can actually honor (a trial, a first deliverable before payment, cancel anytime). No promise of results.
- A clear name for the offer.
- The offer written as a one-paragraph pitch and as a 3-line DM.
- What I should charge, with reasoning tied to the value to the buyer.

Rules: never promise revenue, customers or results for the buyer. Promise what I will deliver and when.
```

**What you get back:** An offer built on a free sample video of their own gym, a 1-hour monthly filming visit so they do nothing, month-to-month terms, named bonuses, a pitch paragraph, a DM and a price recommendation.

## A free thing that feels dumb to give away

**Use it when:** You need a lead magnet people actually want: so useful they wonder why it is free.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: Your business (for example: Google Business Profile setup for local contractors)
- `{{buyer}}`: Your buyer (for example: Roofers and HVAC companies with under 10 staff)
- `{{paid_offer}}`: What you sell afterward (for example: $500 profile and review system setup)

```text
Design a free lead magnet for my business that's so useful people wonder why I give it away.

My business: {{business}}
My buyer: {{buyer}}
What I sell after the free thing: {{paid_offer}}

Rules for the free thing:
- It solves one real problem completely, not a teaser.
- It shows the buyer a second problem they didn't know they had, which my paid offer fixes.
- It's finished work they can use today (a list, an audit of their own business, a sample, a calculator, a template filled in for them), not a PDF of tips.
- I can deliver it in under 15 minutes each, or automate it.

Give me five ideas. For each: what it is, the problem it solves, the problem it reveals, how I deliver it, and why it leads to the paid offer. Rank them.

Then for the top idea write:
1. The headline and one-line promise.
2. The 3 questions or inputs I collect from them.
3. What they get back, section by section.
4. The last line that points to my paid offer, without pressure.
5. Three hooks for posts that drive people to it.
```

**What you get back:** Five ranked ideas (for example a free 10-point audit of their own profile next to their top 3 competitors), plus the headline, inputs, deliverable outline, soft close and three hooks for the winner.

## Price ladder and money model

**Use it when:** You have one offer and want the full path: a way in, a main offer, an upgrade, a smaller option, and a monthly plan.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{main_offer}}`: Your main offer and price (for example: Custom meal plan, $120 one-time)
- `{{buyer}}`: Your buyer (for example: Busy parents who want to lose 10 to 20 pounds)
- `{{costs}}`: Your cost per sale (for example: About 2 hours of my time, $5 in software)
- `{{capacity}}`: Hours you can deliver weekly (for example: 10)

```text
Design a simple money model for my business. One of each, in order, and only things I can deliver.

My main offer and price: {{main_offer}}
My buyer: {{buyer}}
My costs per sale: {{costs}}
Hours I can deliver per week: {{capacity}}

Build:
1. Way in: a free or low-price first step that gets a buyer to try me.
2. Main offer: tighten what I have.
3. Upgrade: something bigger for buyers who want more. When to offer it (the moment they'd want it).
4. Smaller option: for people who say no to the main offer. What I remove to make it cheaper.
5. Monthly plan: what repeats every month so revenue continues.

For each: what it is, price, what it costs me, the margin, and the exact moment I offer it.

Then show the math in a table: if I get 10 buyers into the way in each month, with your best-guess conversion at each step, what lands each month. Label every conversion rate as an assumption I should test. Flag any step that eats too much of my time for my capacity.
```

**What you get back:** A free grocery-list starter, the tightened plan, a monthly check-in upgrade, a template-only downsell and a membership, each priced with margins, plus a 10-buyer math table with assumptions flagged.

## What should I charge?

**Use it when:** You're about to quote a price and you're guessing.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{service}}`: What you sell (for example: Full interior and exterior car detail)
- `{{buyer}}`: Who buys (for example: Commuters and small car dealers)
- `{{time}}`: Time per job (for example: 3 hours plus 30 minutes driving)
- `{{costs}}`: Your costs per job (for example: $18 in products, $6 in gas)
- `{{competitors}}`: Competitor prices (for example: Shop down the road charges $180)
- `{{hourly_target}}`: Your hourly target (for example: $40 an hour)

```text
Help me price this. Show the math.

What I'm selling: {{service}}
Who buys it: {{buyer}}
Time it takes me: {{time}}
My costs (tools, materials, travel): {{costs}}
What competitors charge, if known: {{competitors}}

Work out:
1. My floor: the lowest price where this is still worth my time. Use an hourly target of {{hourly_target}} plus costs.
2. The buyer's value: what this is worth to them (a new customer's value to their business, hours saved, money saved). Show the assumptions.
3. The market range from competitors.
4. A recommended price with a one-line reason, and a "good, better, best" set of three options.
5. How to say the price: frame the cost as the smallest true number (per week, per video, per day), and the value as the biggest true number.
6. What to say when they say "too expensive", three ways, none of them a discount.

Label every assumption. Don't invent competitor prices; say "unknown" if you can't find them.
```

**What you get back:** A floor around $165, the value to a dealer of a car that sells faster, a market range, a recommended price with three tiers, cost framing ("under $6 a week for a monthly plan") and three no-discount answers.

## Sales page that converts

**Use it when:** You need a landing or sales page for your offer, written so a stranger understands it in five seconds.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{offer}}`: The offer and price (for example: Same-week website for local trades, $750, live in 7 days)
- `{{buyer}}`: Who it is for (for example: Plumbers, electricians and handymen with no website or a bad one)
- `{{proof}}`: Real proof you have (for example: 3 sites built for friends, before and after screenshots)
- `{{cta}}`: What they click (for example: Get a free homepage mock-up)
- `{{voice}}`: Your voice (for example: Plain, confident, no jargon)

```text
Write a sales page for this offer. A stranger must understand what it is, who it's for and what to do next within five seconds of landing.

Offer: {{offer}}
Buyer: {{buyer}}
Proof I can show (only real things): {{proof}}
Call to action: {{cta}}
Voice: {{voice}}

Sections, in order:
1. Hero: headline (under 10 words, concrete outcome), subhead (who it's for and how), one button.
2. The problem, in the buyer's words.
3. What you get: 3 to 5 deliverables, each a concrete thing.
4. How it works: 3 steps.
5. Proof: only what I gave you. If I gave nothing, use a demonstration (show the work) instead of testimonials.
6. Objections answered as a short FAQ (price, time, "will this work for me", how to cancel).
7. The offer stack and price, with the risk reversal I can honor.
8. Final call to action.

Rules: no hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, unlock), no invented reviews, numbers or logos, no promises of results the buyer gets. Short sentences. After the page, list the three lines you think are weakest and rewrite them.
```

**What you get back:** A full page draft from hero to FAQ, using your three before-and-afters as proof, plus rewrites of its three weakest lines.

## Objection bank

**Use it when:** Before sales calls or DMs, so you never freeze when someone pushes back.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{offer}}`: What you sell (for example: Monthly lawn care, $140 a month)
- `{{buyer}}`: Who buys (for example: Homeowners in newer subdivisions)
- `{{heard}}`: Objections you already heard (for example: "My kid does it." "I only need it in summer.")

```text
List the objections my buyers will raise and write honest answers I can say out loud.

What I sell: {{offer}}
Buyer: {{buyer}}
Objections I've already heard: {{heard}}

Give me the 12 most likely objections, grouped as: price, timing, trust, "I can do it myself", "need to ask someone", and fit.
For each:
- What they say.
- What they usually mean underneath.
- A short answer (under 40 words) that's honest and builds trust. Ask a question back when that works better than an answer.
- When to walk away instead.

No pressure tactics, fake urgency, or discounts as the default answer.
```

**What you get back:** Twelve grouped objections with what they mean, 40-word answers, question-backs and when to walk away.

## Proposal and scope of work

**Use it when:** A buyer asked "what would it cost?" after a call, or said yes and you need the scope in writing before work starts.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: Your business (for example: Bright Path Bookkeeping, Kemi Adeyemi, monthly books for small trades businesses)
- `{{client}}`: The client and what they told you, in their words (for example: Marco runs a 4-person landscaping crew. "My books are 8 months behind and my accountant needs them by March." He wants it caught up, then kept current.)
- `{{plan}}`: What you will do (for example: Catch up 8 months of books, set up bank feeds and receipt capture, then close each month by the 10th)
- `{{prices}}`: Your prices or range (for example: Catch-up $1,200 one time; monthly $350; payroll add-on $100 a month)
- `{{terms}}`: Timeline and terms (for example: 50% deposit on the catch-up, 2 rounds of questions a month, he sends bank logins and receipts by Nov 1)
- `{{proof}}`: Real proof you can use (for example: Caught up 3 trades businesses this year; one owner quote with permission: "First tax season I didn't panic.")

```text
Write a client proposal and a matching scope of work for this job, ready to paste into a doc.

My business: {{business}}
The client and what they told me, in their words: {{client}}
What I'll do: {{plan}}
My prices or price range: {{prices}}
Timeline and terms (deposit, revisions, what they provide): {{terms}}
Real proof I can use (results, quotes with permission): {{proof}}

PROPOSAL (one to two pages):
1. What you told us: their situation and goal in their own words, two or three sentences.
2. What we will do: three to five steps, each with what they get from it.
3. Included and not included, as a two-column table.
4. Timeline: kickoff, milestones and handover, with dates.
5. Three options (essential, recommended, complete) with what's in each and the price. Make the recommended one the clear choice by what it includes, not by pressure. Say how long the prices are valid.
6. Why us: only the real proof I gave you. If I gave none, leave this section out.
7. Next step: one clear action (reply with the option, or book a time).

SCOPE OF WORK (one page, for after they pick an option):
1. The goal in one sentence.
2. Deliverables, each with what "done" means so both sides can tell it's finished.
3. Not in this project: the things people often assume are included.
4. What the client provides, and by when.
5. Milestones with dates.
6. Revisions included, and how changes get priced in writing before work on them starts.
7. Payment schedule tied to the milestones.
8. A sign-off block with names and dates.

Rules: plain words, nothing I can't deliver, no invented results. Mark any blank I need to fill as [brackets]. This is a business document, not legal advice: if the job is big or risky, tell me to have a lawyer turn the scope into a contract.
```

**What you get back:** A proposal that opens on Marco's March deadline in his words, three options from the $1,200 catch-up to catch-up plus monthly with payroll, and a scope that defines "done" for each month-end close, the Nov 1 logins and the deposit schedule.

**Works in:** Google Docs, Word, Notion
