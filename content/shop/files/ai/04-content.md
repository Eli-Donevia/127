# Content and hooks: get seen by the right people

Find an angle, repeat what works, and turn one idea into a week of posts. These prompts write hooks, scripts, calendars and a voice guide so your content sounds like you.

## Hook bank

**Use it when:** You need scroll-stopping first lines for videos or posts about your business.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: Your business (for example: Meal prep delivery for nurses on night shift)
- `{{audience}}`: Who should watch (for example: Night-shift nurses in my city)
- `{{pain}}`: What they struggle with (for example: Eating vending machine food at 3am, no time to cook)
- `{{story}}`: A true story or proof (for example: My sister is a night nurse and I started cooking for her crew)

```text
Write 30 hooks for short videos about my business. A hook is the first line said or shown in the first 2 seconds.

My business: {{business}}
Who I want watching: {{audience}}
What they struggle with: {{pain}}
Proof or story I can use (only true things): {{story}}

Give 5 hooks in each of these styles:
1. Call out the viewer ("If you own a gym and...").
2. A surprising number or fact (true and sourced, or from my own work).
3. Before and after.
4. A mistake people make.
5. "I tried X so you don't have to" or a demonstration.
6. A strong opinion they'll want to argue with.

Rules: under 12 words each, plain language, no clickbait I can't pay off in the video, no income claims, no "you won't believe". Mark your top 5 and say what the video must show in the first 5 seconds to pay each one off.
```

**What you get back:** 30 hooks in six styles (for example "Night shift nurses: what did you eat at 3am?"), the top 5 marked, and what each video must show first.

## 30-day content calendar

**Use it when:** You want a month of posts planned in one sitting so you stop staring at a blank screen.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: Your business (for example: Math tutoring for grades 6 to 8)
- `{{audience}}`: Who you want to reach (for example: Parents in my school district)
- `{{platforms}}`: Where you post (for example: Instagram and Facebook groups)
- `{{frequency}}`: Posts per week (for example: 4)
- `{{offers}}`: Paid offer and free thing (for example: $45 an hour tutoring; free "where is my kid stuck" 10-minute quiz)

```text
Plan 30 days of content for my business.

Business: {{business}}
Audience: {{audience}}
Platforms: {{platforms}}
Posts per week I can actually make: {{frequency}}
What I sell and the free thing I give away: {{offers}}

Build:
1. Three content pillars with one line each (for example: show the work, teach one thing, behind the scenes).
2. A calendar table: date, platform, pillar, hook, format (talking head, screen recording, before and after, carousel), what to film or design, call to action.
3. Mix: about 70% useful or entertaining, 20% proof and process, 10% direct offers.
4. One weekly "repeat what worked" slot: remake last week's best post in a new way.
5. A batch plan: how to film or make the whole week in one session.

Rules: realistic for one person, no trends that need a big budget, every CTA points to my free thing or a DM keyword.
```

**What you get back:** Three pillars, a 16-post calendar with hooks and formats, a repeat-what-worked slot and a one-session batch plan.

## One idea, ten posts

**Use it when:** You made one good piece of content (a video, a call, a blog post) and want to get ten posts out of it.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{source}}`: Paste the original (for example: (transcript of a 6-minute video on how I price detailing jobs))
- `{{platforms}}`: Where you post (for example: TikTok, Instagram, LinkedIn)
- `{{voice}}`: Your voice (for example: Direct, a little funny, no jargon)

```text
Turn this one piece of content into ten posts.

Source (transcript, post or notes):
{{source}}

Platforms: {{platforms}}
My voice: {{voice}}

Make:
1. Three short video scripts (under 45 seconds each): hook, 3 beats, call to action.
2. Two carousels: slide-by-slide text, 6 to 8 slides each.
3. Two text posts (one story, one list).
4. One email to my list.
5. Two quote graphics: the line and a one-line caption.

Keep each piece standalone. Don't repeat the same hook twice. Note which piece to post first and why.
```

**What you get back:** Three scripts, two carousels, two text posts, an email and two quote graphics, with a posting order.

## Brand voice and look guide

**Use it when:** You want everything you post, write and design to look and sound like one brand.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: Your business (for example: Wedding and event photography)
- `{{audience}}`: Who it is for (for example: Couples 25 to 35 planning small weddings)
- `{{feel}}`: Three words (for example: warm, calm, honest)
- `{{references}}`: Brands you admire (for example: Aesop for calm, Patagonia for honesty)
- `{{samples}}`: Something you wrote (for example: My Instagram bio and last caption)

```text
Write a one-page brand guide for my business so everything I make looks and sounds consistent.

Business: {{business}}
Who it's for: {{audience}}
Three words for how it should feel: {{feel}}
Brands I admire and why: {{references}}
Examples of my writing: {{samples}}

Include:
1. Positioning in one sentence.
2. Voice: we are / we are not table, writing rules, words to use and avoid.
3. Three taglines to choose from.
4. Visual direction: a color palette (hex codes), two fonts (free ones), photo style, and what to never do.
5. Templates to make once: post, story, thumbnail, email header. Describe each layout.
6. A before and after: rewrite one of my samples in the new voice.

Keep it premium and simple. One page.
```

**What you get back:** A one-page guide with positioning, voice rules, tagline options, palette and fonts, template layouts and a rewritten sample.

**Works in:** Canva, Figma

## What is working in my content?

**Use it when:** Once a week or month. Paste your post stats and find out what to double down on.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{posts}}`: Paste posts and numbers (for example: Post | hook | views | saves | shares | follows | DMs / ...)
- `{{goal}}`: What content should do for you (for example: Get DMs from gym owners)

```text
Here are my recent posts and their numbers:

{{posts}}

My goal for content is: {{goal}}

Analyze:
1. My top 3 and bottom 3 posts by the metric that matters for my goal (not just views).
2. What the winners share: hook style, topic, format, length, first frame, posting time. Ask "why did this work when the others didn't" and answer it.
3. Patterns in comments: questions people keep asking (these are my next posts).
4. Three posts to make next week that remake my winners in a new way.
5. What to stop doing.
6. One experiment to run next week, with how I'll know if it worked.

Use my numbers. If the sample is too small to trust, say so.
```

**What you get back:** Winners and losers by DMs, the shared pattern, comment-driven post ideas, three remakes, a stop list and one experiment.

**Works in:** Instagram Insights, TikTok Analytics, Google Sheets

## Email writer

**Use it when:** You need one of the emails every business sends: a launch, a quote follow-up, a review ask, a weekly newsletter, a welcome or a win-back.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{email_type}}`: Which email: launch, quote follow-up, review ask, newsletter, welcome or win-back (for example: Win-back)
- `{{business}}`: Your business and voice (for example: Sol Nail Studio, a two-chair salon in Tucson. Warm, short, a bit playful.)
- `{{audience}}`: Who gets it (for example: Clients who haven't booked in 4 months or more)
- `{{details}}`: What it needs to say (for example: New since then: gel-x extensions, Sunday hours 10 to 3, online booking at solnails.com/book)
- `{{footer}}`: Business name and mailing address (for example: Sol Nail Studio, 214 Example Ave, Tucson, AZ)

```text
Write a {{email_type}} email for my business.

My business and voice: {{business}}
Who gets it: {{audience}}
What it needs to say (details, offer, links, dates): {{details}}
My business name and mailing address, for the footer: {{footer}}

Follow the pattern for the type:
- Launch: what it is, who it's for, what's included, the price and where to see it. Plain, no hype.
- Quote follow-up: check the quote arrived, recap it in two lines, offer to adjust the scope, and make it easy to book a start date or to say no.
- Review ask: thank them for something specific, ask for an honest review with the direct link, and invite them to reply if anything was off. No discount or gift for the review, and send it to every recent customer, not only the happy ones.
- Newsletter: one useful tip, one short story from my week, one small thing to try. Under 250 words.
- Welcome: hello, what I do, how often I'll write and about what, and one easy first step.
- Win-back: thanks, what's new since their last visit, and an easy way back with no pressure.

Give me:
1. Three subject lines, each with a preview line.
2. The email, under 200 words unless it's a newsletter, written like a person, with one clear call to action.
3. A footer with my business name, mailing address and an unsubscribe line for anything sent to a list.
4. A plain-text version.
5. When to send it, and one follow-up if the type needs one.

Use only facts I gave you. No fake deadlines or scarcity, and no results I didn't give you.
```

**What you get back:** Three subject lines (for example "Your chair missed you, and we open Sundays now"), a warm 120-word note naming gel-x and Sunday hours, one "Book online" link, a footer with the address and unsubscribe line, a plain-text copy and a nudge for 5 days later.

**Works in:** Gmail, Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Kit, Flodesk
