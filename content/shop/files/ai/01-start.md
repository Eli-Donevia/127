# Start: pick it and prove it

Use these before you build anything. They test an idea against real demand, find who already sells it, and get you talking to buyers this week.

## The Idea Test

**Use it when:** You have one to five business ideas and want a straight answer on which one to start, before you spend a dollar.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{ideas}}`: Your ideas, one line each: what you sell and to whom (for example: 1. Short promo videos for local gyms / 2. A Shopify store for minimalist gym bags / 3. Math tutoring for middle schoolers)
- `{{about_me}}`: Your skills, experience and anything unfair you have (for example: I edit video in CapCut, I lift 5 days a week, 2K followers on Instagram)
- `{{hours}}`: Hours a week (for example: 12)
- `{{budget}}`: Starting budget (for example: $300)

```text
You are a blunt, experienced founder reviewing my business ideas. No cheerleading. Your job is to stop me wasting a year on the wrong one.

My ideas:
{{ideas}}

About me: {{about_me}}
Time I can put in each week: {{hours}}
Money I can spend to start: {{budget}}

Score each idea 1 to 5 on these seven questions. For each score, write the proof in one line. If there is no proof, the score can't go above 2.
1. Problem: does it solve a problem people already pay money to fix, or add value people pay for (saves time, peace of mind, education)? Name who pays today and what they pay for now.
2. Economics: what does one sale earn after costs? How many sales would cover my monthly costs?
3. Channels: how would the first 25 buyers find me, and what does it cost to get into that channel?
4. Differentiator: why me? Faster, cheaper, better quality, a better model, an unfair advantage I already have?
5. Market direction: is this market growing or shrinking? Cite what you know and say how sure you are.
6. AI impact: is AI making this business easier to run, or making it obsolete?
7. Time to first sale: how many days until a stranger pays me, if I start today?

Then:
- Rank the ideas in a table.
- Pick one winner. Say why in three sentences.
- Name the single biggest risk in the winner and the cheapest way to test it this week.
- Name the kind of insider I should talk to (someone who lives this problem or sells to these buyers) and write the exact message to ask them for 15 minutes.

Rules: be specific to my situation, not generic. If two ideas tie within 3 points, break the tie on time to first sale, then cost to start. Don't ideate new ideas unless all of mine score under 15.
```

**What you get back:** A scored table, one pick (likely the gym videos, since you have the skill and gyms already pay for content), the riskiest assumption, a 7-day test and the message to send an insider.

**Works in:** Web search, Perplexity

## Business match

**Use it when:** You want to start something but don't have an idea yet, and you want three options that fit your skills, time and money.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{skills}}`: Skills and interests (for example: Photography, talking to people, cars)
- `{{city}}`: City or area (for example: Columbus, Ohio)
- `{{hours}}`: Hours a week (for example: 15)
- `{{budget}}`: Starting budget (for example: $500)
- `{{avoid}}`: Anything you want to rule out (for example: Nothing that needs me on calls all day)

```text
Act as a practical business advisor. Suggest exactly three businesses I could start, matched to me.

What I'm good at or enjoy: {{skills}}
Where I live: {{city}}
Hours a week: {{hours}}
Starting budget: {{budget}}
What I don't want: {{avoid}}

Rules for the three:
- At least one is a local or service business (people near me pay me to do something). At least one can run online.
- Each must be something where a beginner can make a first sale within 30 days.
- No business that needs a license I don't have, inventory over my budget, or an audience I don't have yet.

For each business give:
- One-line description: what I sell, to whom, for roughly what price per job or per item.
- Who already pays for this today, and how they find it.
- My first 25: the type of 25 people or businesses I would contact first, and where I'd find them.
- What I'd show them: one sample or mock-up I could make before anyone pays me.
- Startup cost and the tools I'd need.
- Score 1 to 5 on: how painful the problem is, who pays, how easy buyers are to find, growth, and time to first sale.

End with your pick and the first three things I do tomorrow, each under 30 minutes.
```

**What you get back:** Three scored options (for example photos for local car dealers, mobile detailing, a preset pack sold online), each with its first 25, a sample to make, and tomorrow's three moves.

## Five-player teardown

**Use it when:** You picked a business and want to know how the people already winning at it find buyers, price, and deliver.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: What you are starting (for example: Weekly social video packages for independent coffee shops)
- `{{market}}`: Where you sell (for example: Austin, Texas, plus remote clients)
- `{{known}}`: Competitors you already know (for example: @brewreels on Instagram)

```text
Research five businesses or creators already doing what I want to do, and turn them into a playbook I can copy the good parts of.

What I'm starting: {{business}}
Where I sell (city, online, or both): {{market}}
Names I already know of, if any: {{known}}

For each of the five:
- Who they are and what they sell, with prices you can find.
- Where their buyers come from: channels, content formats, offers, lead magnets. Quote real examples.
- What makes them different, in one line.
- What customers praise and complain about (reviews, comments, threads).
- One thing they do that I should copy, and one gap I could beat them on.

Then give me:
1. A comparison table of the five.
2. The three patterns all the winners share.
3. The gap in the market: what nobody is doing well.
4. My positioning line in one sentence, built on that gap.
5. Five things to test in my first 30 days, in order.

Rules: only use facts you can source, and link or name the source. If you can't verify a number, say so. Don't copy anyone's income claims or customer results into my plan.
```

**What you get back:** Five sourced case studies, a comparison table, the shared patterns, the gap, a one-line position and a 30-day test list.

**Works in:** Web search, Perplexity, Apify

## Customer interview kit

**Use it when:** Before you build. You want to learn how buyers solve the problem today and whether they would pay you to solve it.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{business}}`: Your business idea (for example: Pressure washing for restaurants)
- `{{who}}`: Who you will interview (for example: Owners or managers of 10 local restaurants)
- `{{unknowns}}`: What you most need to learn (for example: How often they clean outside, who does it now, what they pay)

```text
Write me a customer interview kit for this business. I want to learn the truth, not hear compliments.

Business: {{business}}
Who I'll interview: {{who}}
What I most need to learn: {{unknowns}}

Give me:
1. A 3-line message to ask for a 15-minute call or visit. Friendly, specific, no pitch.
2. Ten interview questions about their past behavior, not opinions. Start with how they handle this today, what it costs them, what they've tried, and what they'd change. No "would you buy" questions.
3. Three follow-up prompts to dig deeper when they say something interesting.
4. The signals that mean "real problem, will pay" and the signals that mean "polite, won't pay".
5. A one-page note template to fill in after each call.
6. After 10 interviews, how to decide: build, change, or drop it.

Keep my questions short enough to say out loud.
```

**What you get back:** An ask message, ten behavior questions, follow-ups, buy and no-buy signals, a note template and a decision rule.

## Market research brief

**Use it when:** You need the numbers and trends behind a market fast: size, growth, buyers, prices, and where AI is changing it.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{market}}`: The market (for example: Mobile car detailing)
- `{{geography}}`: Where (for example: Mid-size US cities)
- `{{question}}`: The decision you are making (for example: Is there room for one more detailer here, and what would it take to build a steady weekly route?)

```text
Write a research brief on this market. I'll use it to decide whether to go in and how.

Market: {{market}}
Geography: {{geography}}
Question I need answered: {{question}}

Cover:
1. Who buys, how many there are, and what they spend on this today. Give ranges with sources.
2. Is it growing or shrinking, and why. Name the forces.
3. How AI is changing it: what gets cheaper, what gets automated, what new demand shows up.
4. Pricing: what buyers pay at the low, middle and high end, with examples.
5. Channels: where buyers look for this, ranked by how reachable they are for a newcomer.
6. Risks: regulation, licensing, seasonality, platform dependence.
7. The answer to my question, in one paragraph, with your confidence level.

Rules: cite every number. Mark anything older than two years. Say "unknown" instead of guessing. End with three next steps I can do this week.
```

**What you get back:** A sourced brief with buyers, growth, AI impact, price bands, ranked channels, risks and a direct answer with a confidence level.

**Works in:** Web search, Perplexity, Claude Research

## Demand check

**Use it when:** You've had some sales or conversations and want an honest read on whether people really want this, and what to double down on.

**Fill in first:**

- `{{offer}}`: What you sell and the price (for example: Website refresh for local salons, $600 one-time)
- `{{results}}`: Your numbers so far (for example: 60 messages sent, 9 replies, 4 calls, 2 sales, both from Instagram DMs)
- `{{feedback}}`: What customers said (for example: "I just want people to be able to book online." "My nephew made the old one.")

```text
Read my results and tell me honestly whether I have real demand, and what to do more of.

What I sell: {{offer}}
What has happened so far (sales, replies, conversations, numbers): {{results}}
What customers have said, word for word if possible: {{feedback}}

Answer:
1. Demand verdict: strong, early signs, or not yet. Back it with my own numbers.
2. What's working: the channels, messages or buyers that convert best. Ask "why did this work when the others didn't?" and answer it.
3. What to double down on this month, and what to stop.
4. What buyers are really paying for, in their words, versus what I think I'm selling.
5. One change to the offer that would make it easier to say yes to.
6. The number I should track weekly to know if it's getting better.

Rules: don't soften it. If the data is too thin to say, tell me exactly what data to collect in the next 14 days.
```

**What you get back:** A demand verdict, the reason Instagram DMs win, a stop and double-down list, the real job buyers hire you for (online booking), an offer tweak and one weekly number.
